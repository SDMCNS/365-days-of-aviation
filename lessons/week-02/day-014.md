## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**System View Objective**: Explain how ICAO influences aviation without acting as a global aviation regulator for every operation.

ICAO is not a "global FAA"—it has zero field inspectors, issues no pilot licenses, inspects no airplanes, and levies no fines. Under **Article 1**, national airspace sovereignty is absolute. 

Yet ICAO governs global aviation through three structural engines:
1. **Normative Standardization (SARPs)**: 193 Member States negotiate consensus minimum standards across 19 Annexes.
2. **Audit Transparency (USOAP-CMA)**: ICAO audits each national CAA across the 8 Critical Elements (CE-1 to CE-8) of safety oversight. Systemic failure results in a public **Significant Safety Concern (SSC)**.
3. **Third-Party & Market Enforcement**:
   - **Article 33**: Mutual recognition of certificates is revoked if a State falls below ICAO baselines.
   - **Sovereign Bans**: The **EU Air Safety List** (Blacklist) and **US FAA IASA Category 2** ban or freeze non-compliant airlines.
   - **Private Markets**: Reinsurance syndicates (Lloyd's) and aircraft leasing corporations ground aircraft when regulatory legitimacy collapses.

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Scenario**: A developing State cannot afford to recruit or retain senior widebody inspectors, resulting in an ICAO Significant Safety Concern (SSC) and immediate placement on the EU Air Safety List.
- **Institutional Remedy**: The State pools resources with neighboring nations to establish a **Regional Safety Oversight Organisation (RSOO)**, creating a shared, highly qualified inspectorate that resolves protocol questions and restores international market access.
