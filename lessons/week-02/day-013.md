## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Integrate Objective**: Connect international standards to certificates, operational approvals and local procedures.

An ICAO standard in Montreal does not directly command an airliner autopilot. For high-consequence operations—such as landing in dense fog (CAT III autoland) or flying twin-engine airliners across vast oceans (ETOPS)—a complete regulatory chain must link together:
1. **The Global SARP**: ICAO Annex 6 establishes international baseline requirements for all-weather operations, navigation, and extended diversion times.
2. **State Specific Approvals (Part-SPA)**: Transposed into regional/national law, requiring operators to earn specific formal privileges.
3. **Air Operator Certificate (AOC) & Operations Specifications (OpSpecs)**: The formal regulatory attachment to the airline's license explicitly documenting approved capabilities (CAT II/III, ETOPS, RVSM, PBN, Dangerous Goods).
4. **The 4-Pillar Verification Matrix**:
   $$\text{Clearance} = \text{OpSpec Approval} \times \text{Aircraft AFM/MEL} \times \text{Crew Currency} \times \text{Local Aerodrome Safeguards (LVP)}$$
   If any single element is 0, the operation is legally prohibited.

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Scenario**: An aircraft established on an ILS approach at 500 ft AGL in 125 m fog suffers an autopilot channel failure, downgrading capability from Fail-Operational (CAT IIIb, 75 m RVR) to Fail-Passive (CAT IIIa, 175 m RVR).
- **Mandatory Action**: Because the reported weather (125 m) is below the degraded legal minimum (175 m), the crew must immediately execute a **Go-Around**, climb to missed approach altitude, and divert to their alternate aerodrome.
