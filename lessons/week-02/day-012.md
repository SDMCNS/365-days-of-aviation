## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Apply Objective**: Trace a hypothetical international flight across several States and identify the applicable legal interfaces.

An international flight does not traverse a uniform legal territory; it crosses a sequence of distinct sovereign and international domains:
1. **State of Registry (Articles 17–21 & Annex 8)**: Issues the Certificate of Airworthiness (C of A) and radio license, and holds primary penal jurisdiction outside territorial airspace.
2. **State of the Operator (Article 31 & Annex 6)**: Grants the Air Operator Certificate (AOC), approving operations specifications (OpSpecs) and enforcing crew flight time limitations (FTL).
3. **Transit States & Sovereign Airspace (Articles 1, 5, 6)**: National territorial sovereignty extends to 12 nautical miles off the coastline. The aircraft must comply with local AIPs and pay ANSP navigation charges.
4. **The High Seas (Article 12 & Annex 2)**: Beyond territorial waters, no State holds sovereignty. ICAO Annex 2 rules of the air apply without any national deviation or exception.
5. **State of Destination & Foreign Ramp Checks (Articles 16 & 33)**: Authorities exercise border control and ramp safety audits (SAFA / FAA Ramp Checks). While Article 33 protects compliant certificates, serious Category 3 safety hazards result in grounding on foreign soil.

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Scenario**: An aircraft suffers an uncontained engine oil leak mid-ocean at 57°N 030°W and must divert immediately into Reykjavik (BIRK), Iceland.
- **Regulatory Resolution**:
  - **Article 25 (Aircraft in Distress)**: Mandates contracting States to render immediate emergency assistance without prior overflight clearances.
  - **Annex 9 (Facilitation)**: Passengers and crew are cleared into bonded transit facilities without tourist visas.
  - **Article 7 (Cabotage Protection)**: The foreign airline is strictly prohibited from carrying domestic passengers within Iceland.
