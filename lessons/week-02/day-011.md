## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Challenge Objective**: Analyze the legal and commercial collisions that occur when international treaty harmonisation (ICAO SARPs) clashes with sovereign national laws and extraterritorial regulations.

Today's lesson explores:
1. **The Sovereignty Paradox**: Why ICAO provides global baselines, but sovereign States retain the right to impose "super-standards" (Category A differences).
2. **The 1,500-Hour Barrier**: How the US FAA ATP rule creates an entry barrier for international first officers, contrasting with ICAO Annex 1's 250-hour standard.
3. **Extraterritorial Jurisdiction (Regulation EC 261/2004)**: Why European consumer delay compensation applies to non-European airlines departing EU airports, exposing foreign carriers to liabilities of €600 per passenger.
4. **Cabotage Protection (Article 7)**: The legal barrier preventing foreign carriers from operating domestic passenger sectors within another sovereign State's territory.

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Failure Mode 1 (Inadvertent Cabotage Breach)**: Selling domestic tag sectors (e.g. JFK to ORD) to foreign passengers without international flight continuation triggers severe civil fines under Chicago Convention Article 7.
- **Failure Mode 2 (EU261 Extraterritorial Wipeout)**: A technical delay on an outbound European flight forces an international carrier to pay €132,000 in statutory compensation, eliminating the commercial profitability of the route.
- **Failure Mode 3 (Foreign Crew Denial)**: Foreign pilots meeting ICAO standards are denied operational codeshare status due to national super-standards.
