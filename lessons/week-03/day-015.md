## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Understand Objective**: The European Commission, EASA, EU Member States and national competent authorities.

Unlike the monolithic US FAA, the European civil aviation system divides authority across four distinct tiers:
1. **European Parliament & Council**: Enacts primary legislation—the **EASA Basic Regulation (EU) 2018/1139**.
2. **European Commission (DG MOVE)**: Adopts binding secondary "hard law" (Implementing and Delegated Regulations).
3. **EASA (Cologne, Germany)**: Prepares technical opinions, issues soft law (AMC/GM and Certification Specifications), holds direct centralized certification for aircraft designs (Type Certificates) and Third Country Operators (TCO), and audits national authorities.
4. **National Competent Authorities (NCAs / NAAs)**: National civil aviation bodies (DGAC France, LBA Germany, AESA Spain, etc.) that certify airlines (AOCs), license flight crew (Part-FCL), certify maintenance organizations (Part-145), and oversee airports (ADR).
5. **Standardisation & Mutual Recognition**: Under Article 67, certificates issued by any EU authority are automatically valid across all 27 Member States, safeguarded by EASA standardisation audits under Regulation (EU) 628/2013.

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Scenario**: A National Competent Authority fails to resolve an urgent Class G standardisation finding within 15 days, allowing an airline to operate with an unapproved Minimum Equipment List.
- **Escalation Path**: EASA notifies the European Commission, which triggers **Article 70 safeguard measures**, suspending mutual recognition of the NCA's certificates across Europe, and initiates an **Article 258 TFEU infringement procedure** at the European Court of Justice.
