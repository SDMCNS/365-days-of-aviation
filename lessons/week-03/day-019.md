## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Apply Objective**: Trace an operational requirement from EU legislation to an operator's documented process.

Human fatigue impairs pilot decision-making and motor performance. European aviation law governs crew fatigue through a 5-step operational trace:
1. **EU Basic Regulation (EU) 2018/1139 Annex V**: Mandates high-level fatigue risk management obligations.
2. **Implementing Regulation (EU) 965/2012 Part-ORO Subpart FTL**: Enacts hard law limits and establishes the **Commander's Discretion** statutory emergency valve (`ORO.FTL.205(f)`).
3. **EASA Certification Specifications (`CS-FTL.1.205 Table 1`)**: Sets the exact mathematical maximum Flight Duty Period (FDP) based on crew report time, circadian low (WOCL) encroachment, and planned sectors.
4. **Operations Manual Part A (OM-A) Chapter 7**: The airline's approved FTL scheme establishing the exact policy and limits for exercising discretion ($\le 2\text{ hours}$ un-augmented).
5. **Cockpit SOP & Form FTL-104**: The Captain consults crew fitness, signs the Aircraft Tech Log, extends subsequent minimum rest, and submits a formal report to the National Competent Authority within 28 days if discretion exceeds 1 hour.

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Scenario**: An unforeseen delay exceeds the 2-hour discretion ceiling by 30 minutes, and the airline operations controller pressures the crew to depart anyway to avoid missed passenger connections.
- **Commander Authority**: Under **`CAT.GEN.MPA.105`**, the Captain has the non-negotiable legal obligation to refuse departure. Taking off beyond legal duty hours constitutes an illegal flight, voiding hull and third-party insurance and exposing the crew to license suspension.
