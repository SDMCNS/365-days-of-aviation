## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Build Objective**: Create a regulatory traceability table: requirement, source, responsible authority, regulated organisation and evidence.

In aviation regulatory audits, verbal claims have zero standing. Every operational practice must be anchored in an unbroken **5-Point Traceability Chain**:
1. **Operational Requirement**: The explicit technical rule and performance threshold (e.g., 30-min final reserve fuel, max FDP limits).
2. **Statutory Source**: The governing EU implementing rule and paragraph (e.g. `CAT.OP.MPA.181`, `ORO.FTL.205`, `145.A.50`).
3. **Responsible Authority**: The entity legally empowered to audit compliance (e.g. EASA or National Competent Authority).
4. **Regulated Organisation**: The certificate holder legally bound to execute the requirement (e.g. Part-CAT Airline, Part-145 MRO, Aerodrome Operator).
5. **Compliance Evidence Artifact**: The tangible, auditable proof generated during real operations (e.g. signed Operational Flight Plan, Tech Log CRS, Crew Rostering logs, Fire Station readiness sheets).

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Scenario**: EASA publishes an amendment to fuel planning rules with an effective date in 60 days. The airline's internal Management of Change (MOC) process requires 90 days across manual drafting, authority approval, and dispatcher retraining.
- **Regulatory Fallout**: On the statutory effective date, the airline's operations manual is legally invalid, resulting in immediate Category 3 SAFA ramp check groundings on foreign aprons.
