## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Consolidate Objective**: Produce a one-page guide to who makes rules, who oversees compliance and who operates services.

The entire civil aviation industry operates through a tripartite functional architecture:
1. **Pillar 1: Who Makes the Rules? (The Rulemakers)**:
   - **ICAO**: Global consensus Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs).
   - **EU Parliament & Council**: Enacts primary law (EASA Basic Regulation (EU) 2018/1139).
   - **European Commission (DG MOVE)**: Enacts secondary hard law (Implementing Regulations).
   - **EASA**: Drafts technical opinions, issues Certification Specifications (CS), AMC, and GM.
2. **Pillar 2: Who Oversees Compliance? (The Regulators)**:
   - **EASA (Centralized)**: Direct certification for aircraft designs (Type Certificates), Design Organisations (Part-21 DOA), and Third-Country Operators (Part-TCO); standardisation audits of NCAs.
   - **National Competent Authorities (NCAs)**: (DGAC, LBA, AESA, ENAC, CAA) Certifies airlines (AOC), maintenance stations (Part-145), pilots (Part-FCL), and aerodromes (ADR).
   - **Eurocontrol**: Central flow management (ATFM slots) and route charges.
3. **Pillar 3: Who Operates the Services? (The Industry)**:
   - Airlines (Part-CAT), Aerodromes (Part-ADR), ANSPs (Single Sky), Maintenance (Part-145/CAMO), and Flight Schools (ATOs).

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Scenario**: An innovative startup attempts to launch uncrewed autonomous passenger eVTOL flights between cities, claiming that existing airline and pilot rules do not apply.
- **Architectural Safeguard**: The tripartite architecture dynamically responds: EASA creates Special Condition `SC-VTOL`, national authorities require operational authorisations, and passenger operations cannot begin without certified airworthiness and operational oversight, preventing regulatory chaos.
