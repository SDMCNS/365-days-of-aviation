## 2. Core Operational & Technical Concept
**Integrate Objective**: Synthesize how commercial profitability goals (passenger load factors, belly cargo revenue, economic fuel tankering, tight fleet utilization) intersect with physical aircraft limitations and statutory safety rules.

Today's lesson analyzes:
1. **Structural Envelopes vs Commercial Demand**: The three statutory weight limits under CS-25: Maximum Zero Fuel Mass (MZFM, wing root bending), Maximum Take-Off Mass (MTOM, structural & runway limits), and Maximum Landing Mass (MLM, gear shock absorption).
2. **Economic Fuel Tankering & Cost of Carry**: Why carrying cheaper fuel from origin incurs an aerodynamic fuel burn penalty ($\sim 3.8\% / \text{hr}$), and when tankered fuel causes an illegal landing weight exceedance at destination.
3. **High Aircraft Utilization vs Operating Curfews**: How a 30-minute schedule delay on Wave 1 compounds across multi-sector days, triggering crew Flight Duty Period (FDP) breaches and night curfew diversions with catastrophic EU261 compensation liabilities.

## 4. Break It / Stress Test & Safety Hazards
- **Failure Mode 1 (High Density Altitude Take-off Limit)**: A summer heatwave (38°C) reduces engine thrust and climb performance, lowering regulated MTOM by 4,500 kg and forcing immediate offload of tankered fuel and cargo.
- **Failure Mode 2 (Overweight Landing Exceedance)**: An aircraft uplifts 3,000 kg tankered fuel; arrival landing mass exceeds MLM by 27 kg. If landed without burn-off or fuel jettison, mandatory structural inspections ground the aircraft.
- **Failure Mode 3 (Curfew Violation & EU261 Cascade)**: An arrival delayed past a 23:30 night curfew causes diversion, hotel costs, and passenger delay compensation (€250/pax), destroying a month's route profits.

## 5. Regulatory Evidence Record

- **EASA CAT.OP.MPA.181**: Fuel scheme requirements and mandatory reserve protections.
